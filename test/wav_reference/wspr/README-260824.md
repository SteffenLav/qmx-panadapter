# Live reference windows, 2026-08-24 — 20 m, OZ1LAV JO65

Three consecutive dumped windows from the on-device WSPR receiver (dial
14.095600 MHz, QMX, dev Tab5), written by `wspr_rx.c`'s capture dump. Each
`.txt` is the output of **WSJT-X's own `wsprd`** on the same file:

    wsprd -f 14.0956 260824_1906.wav

## Why these exist

`150426_0918.wav` was the only WSPR reference in this tree, and we already
decode it, so it could not measure anything. These three are live, busy windows
with a known-correct answer from the reference decoder — so any change to our
decoder can be scored against them instead of argued about.

## The scoreboard that motivated the next piece of work

| window | `wsprd` | ours | ratio |
|---|---|---|---|
| 19:06 | 11 | 5 | 2.2x |
| 19:10 | 14 | 4 | 3.5x |
| 19:14 | 7  | 1 | 7.0x |
| total | **32** | **10** | **3.2x** |

⛔ **AN EARLIER VERSION OF THIS FILE BLAMED `WSPR_MAX_CANDS`. THAT IS WRONG,
falsified by `test/wspr_cap_sweep.c` on these same three files.** Recorded
rather than quietly edited, because the reasoning error is the lesson.

Lifting the cap from 8 to 24 and trying EVERY candidate, with no cycle-time
limit at all, gains **2 decodes against a 22-decode gap**: 5 / 4 / 3 against
wsprd's 11 / 14 / 7. Mapping wsprd's stations onto our own candidate list for
19:10 says exactly where they go:

| fate | count |
|---|---|
| in our candidate list | **14 of 14** |
| decoded | 4 |
| **tried and FAILED** | **8** |
| beyond the old cap of 8 | 4 |

So **the comb search finds everything and the decoder cannot decode it.** The
bad inference was mine: from "a weak-signal floor cannot skip a strong station
while decoding a weaker one" I concluded the cap must be responsible - a false
dichotomy. Both were true at once: those stations sat beyond candidate 8 AND
they do not decode when tried.

Two real leads came out of it instead:

1. ⭐ **STRONG SIGNALS ARE EXHAUSTING THE FANO SEARCH.** `DD3MS` at -13 dB and
   `DG6RCH` at -16 dB both come back `cycles=1620001`, which is exactly
   `maxcycles_per_bit (20000) x 81 bits` - the sequential decoder ran out of
   budget rather than converging. At -13 dB that is not a sensitivity floor,
   and it points at the soft-metric calibration or the symbol alignment, not at
   raising a limit. This is the highest-value thing to look at next.
2. **NEARBY STATIONS SHARE ONE CANDIDATE.** `G4FBA` (1474 Hz), `PD2LEO` (1475)
   and `PA5CA` (1477) all map to a single candidate at 1473.08. We decode one
   and the other two are unreachable at ANY cap, because there is no separate
   candidate for them. wsprd resolves stations 1-3 Hz apart; our comb search
   produces roughly one candidate per 4-6 Hz cluster and has no successive
   subtraction.

The cap change was still worth making - it costs nothing and recovered 2 of the
19:14 decodes - but it is a footnote, not the fix.

## These windows also FALSIFY the SLOW false-decode guard, externally

`WSPR_GUARD_SLOW_CYCLES` is 250 and is deliberately measured-only. These files
are why it must stay that way — **real stations, confirmed by wsprd, that
needed far more than 250 Fano cycles:**

| station | our cycles | in wsprd's list? |
|---|---|---|
| PA3BCA JO21 (19:06) | **336** | yes |
| G8MCD IO91 (19:06)  | **428** | yes |
| PD2LEO JO21 (19:10) | 246 — four below the threshold | yes |

Against that, `260824_1914.wav` yields `E48XFU` `OA52` at **1987** cycles - a
callsign/grid pairing that is not plausible and which **wsprd does not report at
all** for that window, i.e. a false decode caught in REAL data rather than in
simulation. The simulated fabrication needed 1889.

⚠ **This WEAKENS an earlier claim of mine that "the ranges overlap and no
threshold separates them".** In these three files every wsprd-confirmed station
converged in <= 428 cycles while both fabrications needed >= 1889 - which a
threshold around 1000 would separate cleanly. The counterexample the original
claim rested on is `PA0RDT` at 1345, from a window that was never dumped and so
was never confirmed by another decoder. So the honest position is: SLOW stays
measurement-only because it has never been shown safe, not because it has been
shown impossible. Dumping a window containing a >1000-cycle real station would
settle it.

## Provenance

Written by the device, header byte-identical to `150426_0918.wav`'s (checked by
`test/wspr_wav_harness.c`). 2,880,044 bytes each: 44-byte canonical PCM header
plus 120.000 s of 12 kHz mono 16-bit. Peak input levels 90.3 / 83.5 / 107.5,
i.e. a strong, busy band — not a marginal night.

## Where the 32-vs-12 gap actually is (all measured, 2026-08-24 evening)

Four levers tested with `test/wspr_cap_sweep.c` against these files. Three of
them are NOT the limit, which is worth as much as knowing what is:

| lever | result |
|---|---|
| candidate cap 8 -> 24 | +2 of a 22-decode gap |
| Fano budget 20x (20000 -> 400000/bit, 32.4 M cycles) | **0 - identical 12** |
| hard-metric mismatch -2..-8 | flat, +-1 |
| weighted-metric ALPHA 2/4/6/10 | 11 / 15 / 14 / 15 - noise, and it MOVES decodes between files rather than adding them |

⭐ **The Fano search is UNABLE, not slow.** Twenty times the cycle budget finds
nothing extra, so the failures are paths the metric cannot resolve rather than
paths it ran out of time on. That kills "raise the limit" as an idea outright.

⭐ ALPHA=4 scored 15 against the current 6's 14 and did not regress the WSJT
reference - but one decode across three files is noise, and volatility of that
shape is what a decoder sitting AT its threshold looks like. **Deliberately not
adopted**: the docs already record a metric change that passed a synthetic sweep
and regressed real WAVs 5/8 -> 1/8. One decode is not evidence.

**So the gap decomposes into two STRUCTURAL items:**

1. **NO SUCCESSIVE SUBTRACTION - worth ~5-7 decodes here.** 9 of wsprd's 32
   decodes sit within 6 Hz of another station, and our comb yields ~one
   candidate per 4-6 Hz cluster, so at most one of each is reachable. Sometimes
   NEITHER is: `DK8AF` (-16) and `DD3MS` (-13) are 4 Hz apart at 19:10 and we
   decode neither, because the single candidate between them sees a mixture.
   ⚠ That also explains an anomaly used earlier as evidence: DD3MS failing at
   -13 dB while OE5MMP decodes at -18 is NOT a floor violation, it is
   interference. wsprd decodes a signal, SUBTRACTS it, and re-searches; we have
   no such pass.
2. **WEAK-SIGNAL CAPABILITY below about -18 dB.** wsprd works down to -29 dB on
   these files; our decodes run -11 to -18. An ~11 dB gap, wider than the
   -22.7-vs--29 figure in the docs. This is where soft-metric QUALITY matters,
   and where Phase 1's two failed attempts live - read them before a third.

Do (1) first: it is bounded, measurable against these files, and needs no
tuning-by-guesswork.

## ⚠ TWO CORRECTIONS FROM THE LIVE RUN (2026-08-24, 20:40-20:52 UTC)

**1. A HOST BENCHMARK OF A HOT LOOP IS NOT EVIDENCE ABOUT THIS DEVICE.**
The low-pass measured +5 % on the laptop (241 -> 253 ms). On the P4 it was a
**5x slowdown**: candidates tried per cycle fell 15 -> 3, `skipped (budget)`
17 of 20, and decode yield collapsed. Two causes, both invisible on a host:
`double` accumulators (the P4 FPU is SINGLE precision, so double is a software
library - CLAUDE.md already records 80 s vs 2.8 s for the same synthesis) and
`% 255` per tap, which is an integer division. Switched to `float` and a
power-of-two ring (`& 255`): `skipped` 17 -> **6**, against 5 before the filter
existed, so the filter costs about ONE candidate of throughput. Decode output on
the reference files is byte-identical to the double version, so the accuracy
never needed it.

**2. A REAL STATION NEEDED 826 FANO CYCLES**, live: `G8MCD IO91 23 dBm
cycles=826` - the same G8MCD that appears in all three reference windows here.
That **breaks the "a threshold around 1000 would separate them cleanly"**
suggestion written above: 826 real against the 1014 fabrication is far too tight
to threshold safely. The original position stands after all - SLOW stays
measurement-only, and this is now evidence rather than caution.
