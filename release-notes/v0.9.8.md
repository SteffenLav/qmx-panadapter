## Highlights
The waterfall-scroll jumpiness from v0.9.6/v0.9.7 is **fixed**. One line changed: render task target rate dropped from 30 Hz to 10 Hz. The bug was an LVGL flush cascade, not a DSP issue.

## What's changed
### Render task rate: 30 Hz to 10 Hz
`RENDER_PERIOD_MS` in `main/render/render.c` raised from 33 to 100. The displayed update rate is now 10 Hz, matching what the system was actually achieving anyway.

## Root cause
At 30 Hz target, render_task was overrunning every iteration (work ~80 ms vs. 33 ms budget). `vTaskDelayUntil` ran in catch-up mode constantly, leaving zero idle gap between iterations. Each `display_unlock()` call priority-inherited the LVGL flush task, but render_task immediately reacquired the lock before LVGL could settle, accumulating dirty regions across iterations. The result was bimodal flush times -- about 47 ms for partial-region flushes, about 73 ms for full-screen flushes when dirty regions accumulated -- producing the visible irregular ~1 Hz jump.

At 10 Hz target, the ~68 ms idle gap per cycle gives LVGL clean time to flush small dirty rects between iterations. Unlock cost stabilizes at ~26 ms. Render period is flat at 100 ms. No visible jump.

## Honest caveats
This is a workaround, not a deep fix. The underlying mechanism (priority-inherited LVGL flush on `display_unlock()` with PSRAM ~30 MB/s bandwidth limiting framebuffer transfer) is still there. Raising the rate above 10 Hz will likely bring back the cascade. A proper fix would need investigation of `esp_lvgl_port`'s lock/unlock implementation -- flagged as future work.

The diagnosis path that finally cracked this: bracketing instrumentation (function-entry timestamp, lock-acquire timestamp, lock-release timestamp, function-exit timestamp) on `ui_push_spectrum` and `ui_push_waterfall_row`. Splitting the 80 ms function wall-clock into acquisition wait + lock-held work + post-unlock time isolated `display_unlock()` itself as taking 47-73 ms, which made the LVGL flush cascade obvious.
